# codexLuna v0.1 3D — design decisions

1. **3D open field:** The measured `TRACK_SEGS` loop is the comparison route, while the surrounding grass, side roads, trees, houses, and rocks are freely roamable space. Leaving the road changes grip and fuel consumption, not the evaluation axes.
2. **Camera:** The default is a third-person chase camera matching the reference feel. `C` toggles a wider photo-orbit view without changing physics or scoring.
3. **Drift:** Drift remains `clutch-latched`: a deliberate 0.22-second hold plus speed and steering is required to enter; releasing the clutch recovers in a fixed 0.36-second window. Tap and ordinary steering do not enter drift.
4. **Asset pass:** Blender 5.1.2 is now available. The product track has authored GLB LOD0/1/2 files for KART-01, PLANE-01, TRUCK-01, BOAT-01, and TRAIN-01, with editable .blend sources. The separate codexLuna-product-garage-v0.html prototype loads each LOD0 without changing the competition physics or QA contract.
5. **Miniature travel read:** The chase camera is now a higher, longer diorama view with a controllable 14–58m distance range. A CSS miniature lens puts the visual focus in a central band, while four low-contrast horizon landmarks make the world feel traversable rather than like a flat road strip. `Z`/`X` and the mouse wheel change distance during driving; `C` still toggles photo orbit.
6. **Hero car redesign:** The car is treated as a compact field-scout rally wagon rather than a generic block. The body now has a tapered nose, shoulder changes, a shortened cabin, rear cargo volume, separate window planes, wheel cylinders with hubs, fender blocks, grille, lamps, mirrors, roof rails, and a light bar. This remains an inline low-poly prototype; a commercial-ready asset still needs authored normals/materials and an art-direction pass in a real 3D package.
7. **Wheel contact correction:** The first hero pass placed the tire centers too far inside the body width and made the tire radius too small relative to the body. The corrected draw pass moves the wheel centers outward to ±3.12 local X, increases the radius to 0.98, places the bottom at Y≈0.06 against the road at Y≈0.02, and moves the fender blocks to the same lateral track.
8. **Product-reference pass:** The next design pass derives principles from official product descriptions, not copied names, logos, or silhouettes: Jimny's unmistakable front grammar and trapezoid wheel arches; WRX's flared fenders and functional vents; 911 Dakar's high-sidewall off-road stance, protective sills, towing eyes, and disciplined two-tone rally accent. The implementation adds five grille slots, round lamps, side vents, protective guards, and front tow eyes while keeping the vehicle original.
9. **Travel integration:** The authored KART-01_LOD0.glb is now mounted into a separate product travel slice. The route, 60Hz physics, NUT handling, drift contract, camera distance, photo orbit, and T1-T7 self-QA remain operational; the competition HTML stays frozen.
10. **Product capture pass:** The product travel slice adds three route-facing landmarks (rest stop, destination silhouette, route markers) and a query-driven HUD-free capture mode for store-image review. These are product-track additions and do not change the competition artifact.
11. **Map presentation pass:** The travel slice now has a navigational route map with current position, route markers, destination, and river overlay. The world layer mirrors it with continuous river geometry, a road bridge, and distant hill masses; a demo-only route assist is available through the local demo query for visual review.
12. **Authored scenery kit:** The product track now owns `OPEN-FIELD-SCENERY-01` as a Blender-authored LOD0/1/2 kit. Its river, bridge, rest stop, destination beacon, hills, and route markers use the same travel-world coordinates as the route map, and the travel slice loads LOD0 with the previous procedural layer retained as an async fallback.
13. **Product feedback layer:** The travel slice now uses offline Web Audio only after a user gesture. A low-volume engine oscillator follows speed and throttle, wind follows speed, and short tones mark drift entry/recovery and NUT collection. Audio is presentation feedback and does not enter physics, scoring, or deterministic simDrive.
14. **Store capture composition:** `?capture=1` removes the editorial shell, HUD, controls, and page scroll, expands the game to the full viewport, and starts at 18m camera distance so the authored KART-01 remains readable in a store-style frame. The normal 14–58m camera range remains unchanged during play.
15. **Vehicle heading handoff:** Physics yaw is the world forward vector `(cos(yaw), sin(yaw))`. Blender KART-01 presents its forward axis as local GLB `+Z`, so the renderer uses `Math.PI / 2 - yaw`; the pre-load procedural fallback uses the same heading. This prevents sideways-looking acceleration and makes chase-camera direction agree with the visible vehicle.
16. **Motion readability pass:** A compact route ramp at `JUMP_S = totalLength * 0.18` launches a centered car above the `24 m/s` threshold with fixed `12.5 m/s` vertical velocity and `30 m/s²` gravity; landing is deterministic and exposed in the HUD. Drift now leaves fading twin tire marks and dust, while the authored GLB receives velocity-based slip yaw, side roll, drift squash, airborne stretch, and landing impact squash. These are presentation layers over the same fixed 60Hz physics and do not alter the competition artifact.
17. **Raindrop benchmark / velocity-art pass:** Rondo comparison against the owner's `ゲーム用` Raindrop benchmark identified a system-level gap rather than a missing feature: vehicle, terrain, motion FX, competition context, and HUD were not speaking one visual language. The separate product travel slice now uses true alpha-blended contact shadows, face-normal directional/cel lighting, speed-responsive chase distance and FOV, subtle drift roll and impact shake, deterministic speed rays/dust/landing pulse, denser ridge-led scenery, and a translucent GLB ghost driven by a position-sequence replay. An attempted box-cloud layer was rejected after browser review because it increased prototype read. No benchmark asset or code was copied.
18. **Product claim boundary:** The pre-elevation travel slice passed browser T1-T7 and the 58 FPS regression line with no console errors, but those machine results do not establish commercial art quality and are not reused as current performance proof after geometry changes. The owner retains the final impression gate; terrain/material depth, production sound, multi-rival staging, fresh elevation-build capture, and store-capture polish remain before a Steam-ready claim.
19. **Traversable high-relief terrain:** The product travel slice now owns a closed 0–30m route elevation profile, 128-sample elevated road/shoulder ribbons, and a 56×56 triangulated terrain mesh. `terrainHeight` shapes and lowers the natural ground beneath the road; `surfaceHeight` is the authoritative wheel/contact surface for the car, camera target, NUTs, drift marks, dust, and ghost. A pure Node harness samples the rendered terrain/road relationship, preserves positive road clearance, and verifies deterministic drive, drift recovery, jump landing, and NUT non-respawn. This product-only pass does not modify the frozen competition HTML.
20. **Rejected-drift rebuild:** Owner review rejected the prior drift and overall visual language as cheap. Browser screenshot review confirmed five actionable causes: excessive body/camera roll, spherical paved-road dust, central radial streaks, unvaried terrain/road color, and large artificial cone/pyramid mountains. The product slice now steers named KART-01 front-wheel nodes, keeps all vehicle parts on one pose root, connects tire marks from actual successive rear contact points, limits road drift to tire marks with off-road-only dust, uses edge-only motion streaks, and adds deterministic terrain/road material variation plus mixed roadside forms. Final browser evidence is T1-T7 PASS, 60 FPS / 16.9ms worst / zero over 33ms, bench PASS at 1135.72 simulated FPS / 3.4ms maximum / zero over 33ms, and no console warnings or errors. This is accepted as a stronger prototype pass, not a Steam-ready claim.
21. **Manual jump restored from the product specification:** The competition v0 is deliberately narrower and does not define jumping. The RALLY WORLD project brief's document 49 and the existing control/jump prototype define the product behavior: the player chooses the jump, may use common air pitch/yaw/roll control, and spends fuel while controlling the vehicle in air. The travel slice therefore binds jump to `J` and a visible `JUMP` action, uses rising-edge gating, and forbids automatic ramp launch. A ramp only multiplies a chosen jump. No landing reward or NUT rule is added without a product judgment gate.
22. **Blender world-axis correction:** Game-space Y-up positions had been authored directly into Blender's Z-up scene, which produced floating and crossing scenery after GLB export. The scenery generator now maps game `(x, y, z)` to Blender `(x, -z, y)`, maps dimensions consistently, and applies yaw on Blender Z. The rebuild also replaces the oversized start crossbar with two low pylons and regular cone mountains with irregular terraced ridge meshes. This repairs placement and silhouette without changing physics, scoring, or the frozen competition artifact.

Reference sources: Suzuki Jimny design (https://www.suzuki.co.jp/car/jimny/styling/), Subaru WRX features (https://www.subaru.com/vehicles/wrx/2025/features.html), Porsche 911 Dakar highlights (https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/911-Dakar/Highlights.html).

The renderer is original direct WebGL with procedural low-poly geometry. No Three.js bundle, B2 implementation code, B2 tuning values, external asset, or network request is included.
